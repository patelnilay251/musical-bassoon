# Vacant Sunlight

*An algorithmic philosophy for a summer that is computed rather than painted.*

A summer afternoon is a deterministic system. Given a latitude, a date and an hour, the sun has exactly one position, and given the sun, every shadow has exactly one shape. Vacant Sunlight treats the resort illustration as the end of that chain of consequences. Nothing in the frame is placed by hand except the rules: the world is built from geometry, the light comes from solar astronomy, and the image is resolved one sample at a time by a renderer written from first principles. The aim is a meticulously crafted algorithm in which every constant has been weighed and re-weighed, so that the result reads as effortless: flat, bright and inevitable.

Rules come before drawings. The architecture grows on a one-meter grid. Volumes stack, the upper floor cantilevers toward the water only as far as its columns allow, and the eaves overhang just enough to throw one hard line across a white wall. The pool takes its proportions from the facade it faces. Palms are grown rather than drawn: a trunk rises straight and then gives in to its lean, fronds arc out and droop under a gravity term, and leaflets taper along each rib. Seeded randomness varies every choice within tight tolerances, so each seed is a different villa in the same summer. This is the product of painstaking tuning: variation that never looks random.

Flat color is a lighting model, not a filter. Every planar face resolves to a single tone: its local color under warm sun, or the same color under cool sky when it turns away or falls into shadow. There are no outlines and no textures, and edges exist only where two flat fields meet. Shadows are hard because a light source at infinite distance makes them hard, and they are blue because the only light left inside them comes from the sky. Gradients are reserved for things that are gradients in life: the sky, deep water, the slow warming of light across the day. This restraint is where the craft hides. A master-level implementation lets the engine do less, precisely.

Water is the one place where the world looks back at itself. The pool is solved rather than faked: rays refract into a tiled basin and lose red light with depth, everything above the surface is rendered a second time from a mirrored camera, a Fresnel term decides how much of each the eye receives, and a net of caustic light is laid across the floor. The ripple field is a deterministic function of the hour, so the same moment always shimmers the same way.

Time is the only brush that moves. A single parameter, the hour, sweeps the sun along its real path from dawn to night. The sky's palette follows the sun's elevation, shadows lengthen and turn, the sea picks up a glittering path under a low sun, windows and pool lights take over after dusk, and stars come out of the navy. Because the world persists, time becomes narrative: the same hotel, from any angle, at any hour, which is what lets these frames become a book.

The subject is absence. Nobody appears. People are implied only by what they leave behind: a folded towel, an opened umbrella, a float drifting across the deep end, a car that is there in the morning and gone by night. The emptiness should feel like a held breath rather than a missing figure. Achieving that takes deep computational patience, with every parameter tuned so that the finished frame looks like it took no effort at all.

## Second movement: a town, painted

The first engine rendered a resort, and it looked rendered: correct, continuous and generic. A painter does not solve for light; they decide it. So light is now chosen. Every moment names its tones, planes take one of three values, walls are sprayed lighter toward the sun, and skies are laid down the way an airbrush lays them: a blue ground, white mist up from the horizon, blue back over it, never perfectly even. Glass is painted as glass is painted, dark at the foot of each floor with bands of reflected light slanting across. Water is a mirror first and a surface second, and gets its bands and crest lines last, like highlights on a finished board.

Composition matters as much as color. Paintings of this kind are made with a level eye: verticals stay vertical, horizons sit low, and the sky is given room. The camera is built that way, level with a rising front, and each place is framed deliberately rather than orbited. The frame crops buildings the way a painter would. Palms lead the eye up, and a road leads it down to the sun.

One house became a town, because variety is how a style proves it is a style: a motel with a neon sign, a boulevard of fan palms aimed at the midsummer sunset, a beach, a marina with a lighthouse. The subject is still absence. Now the absence travels. One visitor, never seen, keeps a schedule across the whole town, and the site and the book share it. A towel on the sand, a gap in the surfboard rack, an empty slip where a red sloop should be, and a yellow convertible back at the motel under NO VACANCY.
